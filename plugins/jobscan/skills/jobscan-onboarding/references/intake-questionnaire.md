# Intake questionnaire

Plain-language version for prospective users: `docs/INTERVIEW-QUESTIONS.md` — edit both when questions change.

## Start with what already exists — do not open with question 1

Asking someone to recite a career they already wrote down is the fastest way to lose them. Before the
interview begins, ask for material, in this order:

1. **Their CV or résumé.** "Drag it into the chat, or just tell me where it is — a Word file, PDF, or plain
   text all work." Offer to read it from disk if they name a folder. This one file answers roughly 19 of the
   44 questions below.
2. **Anything else that already answers these questions** — an old profile or biosketch, a personal
   statement, a LinkedIn export, a grant biosketch, past cover letters (also the best raw material for the
   voice file), or a worksheet they filled in ahead of time. Take answers in any form: a Word document, a
   Pages document, an email to themselves, a photo of handwritten notes.
3. **Their preference for how to proceed.** Some people would rather answer everything themselves in one
   pass. Offer it plainly: "I can pull most of this from your CV and just check it with you, or I can give
   you the whole list to answer at your own pace — which do you want?" If they choose the full list, give
   them all 44 questions grouped as below and let them answer in their own time and format.

If they have no CV and no written material, run the interview as a plain conversation — but say once that a
CV would cut the time roughly in half, in case one exists that they didn't think to mention.

## Reading the CV: pre-fill, then confirm

- **Draft an answer for every `[CV]` question below** from what you read. Do not ask those questions.
- **Confirm in batches, not one at a time.** "From your CV I have: 12 publications, $1.4M in funding, six
  years supervising a four-person lab. Anything wrong or missing?" beats twelve separate questions.
- **Never write an inference into `profile.md` as fact.** Everything read from a CV is provisional until the
  user confirms it. Where they don't confirm, mark it and move on rather than quietly keeping it.
- **CVs omit numbers.** They list a role but not its scale, a grant but not the amount, "supervised graduate
  students" but not how many. Every `[CV]` answer still needs a numbers pass: ask for the missing figure,
  never invent or estimate one.
- **CVs undersell non-academic work.** Section D and question 24 exist because people delete the jobs that
  prove the transferable skills. Ask about them even when the CV shows nothing.

## Revise the remaining questions before you ask them

The list below is a checklist of what you need to know, not a script to read aloud. Once you've read the CV,
rewrite what's left:

- **Use their vocabulary.** Ask about "your extension work" or "your time at the diagnostic lab," not "your
  outreach portfolio."
- **Drop what doesn't apply.** No publications on the CV → skip the DOI-split question. Never held a grant →
  skip the funding questions. Not academic → skip the dissertation framing question entirely.
- **Sharpen where the CV is ambiguous** instead of asking the generic version. Not "how many publications?"
  but "your CV lists 12 — are all 12 published with DOIs, or are some still in review?"
- **Anchor preference questions to real evidence.** Not "what's your salary floor?" in the abstract, but
  "your last posted role was a GS-12 — is that the floor, or would you go lower for the right job?"
- **Ask conversationally, a few at a time.** Never dump the list at someone who didn't ask for it.

## Legend

- **`[CV]`** — draft this from their CV and confirm it. Only ask if there's no CV or it's silent.
- **`[ASK]`** — a CV cannot answer this. Always ask.
- **`[AUTO]`** — you detect this yourself. Don't ask unless detection fails.

Capture real numbers. Flag any missing metric and ask a follow-up rather than inventing one. Examples are
field-neutral — adapt them to the user's discipline.

## A. Identity, positioning, constraints
1. `[CV]` Name, city/state (or country), email, phone, LinkedIn, any portfolio/lab site.
2. `[CV]` Current title and employer; career stage (grad student, postdoc, early-career, established
   pivoting, …).
3. `[CV]` In 1–2 sentences: how do you want to be positioned? What throughline should every application
   communicate? *(Draft this from the CV and offer it back for correction — most people find it far easier
   to react to a proposed positioning than to compose one.)*
4. `[ASK]` The failure mode to avoid — what shouldn't applications collapse into (the narrow identity that
   undersells your real value)?
5. `[ASK]` Work authorization: citizen/PR, visa status, countries needing sponsorship?
6. `[ASK]` Countries/regions you'd relocate to even without authorization, if sponsorship were offered?
7. `[ASK]` Location preferences (urban/rural, political lean, regions to prioritize/avoid). Are remote roles
   exempt?
8. `[ASK]` Salary floor (absolute min), preferred/target range, higher floor if relocation required.
9. `[ASK]` Government pay-scale floors to respect (min grade/band)?
10. `[ASK]` Role types, sectors, or employers to explicitly avoid, and why (your "avoid-list").

## B. Headline accomplishments (numbers)
11. `[CV]` Total grant/funding secured (aggregate) and the single largest award (amount, funder, project,
    your role).
12. `[CV]` Large projects/programs led — scale (samples, plots, datasets, years, partner organizations).
13. `[CV]` Supervisory/mentoring — how many people, over what period, doing what.
14. `[CV]` Publications: total, **published/DOI'd separated from in-review**. Confirm: never list in-review
    as published? (Recommend yes.)
15. `[CV]` Presentations/outreach — how many, audience sizes, languages, standout engagement numbers.
16. `[CV]` Awards/scholarships/honors — with amounts/rankings.
17. `[CV]` Certifications/licenses relevant to the field.

## C. Skill inventory
18. `[CV]` Technical/lab skills and tools.
19. `[CV]` Data/analysis tools (R, Python, pipelines, stats, GIS) — list from the CV, then ask them to grade
    each one honest strength vs. ramp-up. The list is on the CV; the honesty isn't.
20. `[ASK]` Hands-on generative-AI/LLM experience (tools, uses)?
21. `[CV]` Project/program management (budgets, grant admin, team leadership, protocols).
22. `[CV]` Regulatory/compliance (permits, biosafety, import/export, licensing).
23. `[CV]` Communication/teaching/outreach; multilingual ability.
24. `[ASK]` "Hidden" transferable skills from non-academic jobs worth reframing.

## D. Roles & experience
25. `[CV]` Each position (reverse chronological): title, employer, location, dates, 3–6 quantified bullets.
    The CV gives the positions; the *quantification* usually has to be asked for, bullet by bullet.
26. `[ASK]` Earlier/non-academic jobs worth keeping for transferable-skill evidence — the ones trimmed off
    the CV.

## E. Leadership, service, framing
27. `[CV]` Elected/appointed positions, committees, society involvement.
28. `[ASK]` Negotiation/bargaining/advocacy experience.
29. `[ASK]` How do you describe your dissertation/postdoc now? How should it read to a non-specialist
    recruiter?
30. `[CV]` Which field terms need plain-language translation vs. which should stay because target roles
    expect them? *(Pull the jargon out of their CV, propose the translation table, have them correct it.)*

## F. Track record & voice
31. `[ASK]` Past successful applications/interviews/offers — what specifically worked?
32. `[ASK]` Real past cover letters or interview transcripts that landed well? (Best raw material for the
    voice file — ask them to share the files, don't ask them to describe them.)
33. `[ASK]` Interview questions you've been asked that reveal what employers screen for?
34. `[ASK]` Writing preferences: tone, sentence-length comfort, liked/avoided phrasings, hard "never do
    this" rules, and any minimum font size for documents.

## G. Target roles & search scope
35. `[ASK]` Job titles/sectors you're aiming for. *(Propose a list from their CV first — this is the input
    the scanner's title matching is built from, so a vague answer here degrades every later scan.)*
36. `[CV]` Domain keywords for search — and flag any **asymmetric pairs** that must both be searched
    separately. Draft these from the CV's vocabulary and confirm.
37. `[ASK]` Geographic scope (states, international regions, remote-only?) and any domestic/international
    split quota.
38. `[ASK]` Adjacent sectors to screen out.

## H. Interview story seeds
39. `[ASK]` 3–5 real anecdotes (a success, a failure/lesson, a conflict resolved, a mentoring moment,
    explaining something technical to a non-expert) → STAR answers. A CV can point at *where* the stories
    live ("you led that multi-state survey — what went wrong on it?"), but only they can tell them.

## I. Tooling & environment
40. `[AUTO]` Runtime (Claude Code CLI, Desktop, claude.ai) and whether Skills are available — you already
    know this. Don't ask.
41. `[AUTO]` Firecrawl connected? Check for the tool rather than asking. If it's missing, offer it in one
    sentence and connect it yourself — the keyless hosted server needs no account and no API key (Step 7 has
    the command). If they decline, name the fallback once and move on. Never send them off to sign up for
    anything mid-interview.
42. `[AUTO]` Browser tool available (for JS portals)? Check, don't ask.
43. `[ASK]` **Word or Pages?** One question, and it only changes which two-line instructions you give when
    they need to edit a file themselves. Both work. Nothing to install.
44. `[ASK]` Where should your job-search files live, and do you want the search to run automatically each
    week? Ask in plain terms — "your Documents folder is a good default" — and convert their answer into a
    real path yourself. Never ask a non-technical user to type a path or explain what `~` means.
